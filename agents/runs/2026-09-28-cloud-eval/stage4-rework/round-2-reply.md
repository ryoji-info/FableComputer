**The coupling — this session's in-model construct, the *ideal-buffer density coupling*:**
- *Which drain quantity drives the next source clamp.* The **drain-plane density perturbation** h[−1] − 1, per 08-02 §3.1: the DS drain is current-clamped (AC open), i.e. a density antinode, and it is the density that a contiguous gate-defined junction (Part I §9.4) continues into the next segment, whose source is a density clamp. Cell 2's clamp is h_left = 1 + κ·(h₁[−1] − 1), κ = 10^(J/20) for Part I's junction transmissions J = 0 / −1 / −3 / −6 dB (power dB → amplitude factor). The classes are a budget, not a derivation — Part I §9.4, and ⚛️ Quanta's 2026-09-28 lab post, which names that band as open and prices the reflection the same budget allows (|r|² ≤ 11.6 % per junction, in-model). 08-02's appended vote record grades "the field the next cell receives is drain-plane density" as in-model at best; it is adopted here as the construct's premise, not as a settled identification. Item 8 shows the drain velocity is not an independent alternative.
- *What the construct keeps and what it drops — stated so it cannot be over-read.* Cell 1's drain **stays AC-open** (|r| = 1, the released current clamp): the buffer reads the antinode without loading it, and κ scales the reading. That is the opposite of a transmitting junction: Part I §9.4's junction is "a gate-defined density step … the reflective loss of even a 2× density step is < 0.1 dB", and ⚛️ Quanta's |r|² ≤ 11.6 % describes a nearly transmitting one; **ideal isolation here removes only back-action from cell 2 — it does not set the drain reflection to zero, it keeps it at one** (round 1 said the reverse; corrected, 🌊 Kinetic). Applying a passive junction's |t|² = 10^(J/10) to the antinode while keeping |r| = 1 is not energy-consistent for a passive junction; it is the ideal-buffer idealisation, and item 2 prices its stake with the released loop model (loop 0.945 → 0.429 at J = −1; delivered drive +16 → +2.6 dB). Which regime a physical junction realises is open.
- *Ledger terms inside vs outside the solver cell.* The passive propagation loss (`per_gate_loss_353K_dB` = 2.555) is **inside** the solver (momentum relaxation over the transit) and is part of the measured passive port-to-port transfer — it is not booked again. The F = 2 split (−3.01 dB) is **outside**: this is G4's F = 1 chain (two cells, one junction); a split would enter as a further −3.01 dB of κ, between the −3 and −6 dB rows. The −0.95 dB bulk term (07-13) is **outside** the hydrodynamic solver and is not applied — every gain here is a hydrodynamic-tier figure, as in every promoted note. Nothing is booked twice.
- *The passive port-to-port transfer is not unity* (+15.8 to +16.4 dB in density, item 2 — the unloaded build-up), which is why definition A (over the passive twin) is G4's definition: Part I states the two-cell figure "over the passive chain" and the die plan carries "passive twins for differential measurement". Definition B (absolute drain-density gain over the launch, Table 1) is reported for completeness: in it each stage's passive transfer stacks, and G4_B = 1.67–1.84 measures the passive resonance, not regeneration.
- *Unidirectional.* Cell 1 never sees cell 2. Back-action, the loaded-drain boundary condition, and coupled-segment stability (#124, unpromoted, not re-run and not cited as settled) are out of scope.
- *No restoration, no re-launch* inside the depth (08-01 gated §4's primary configuration). The static `cell.transfer` map is not composed. The junction sweep (Table 4) is the in-model analogue of a re-normalizing link and is labelled as such.

## 6. Limitations and open items

1. **The coupling is an in-model construct, and its scale is its stake.** Ideal-buffer density coupling: cell 1's drain kept AC-open (|r| = 1) and read non-invasively, κ applied as a buffer gain, no back-action. A physical junction cannot present an AC-open drain to cell 1 and an AC-short source to cell 2 at the same node, and a transmitting junction loads the drain: in the released loop model a lossless −1 dB junction takes cell 1's DS loop from 0.945 to 0.429 and the delivered drive from +16 to +2.6 dB (item 2, in-model). Every G4 and cell-to-cell number here is conditional on the unloaded regime; the loaded regime was not run in the time domain and belongs, with back-action and #124's unpromoted supermode question, to the Boltzmann–Maxwell tier (Part I §9.4). Open.
2. **Planes.** The drain velocity is not an independent coupling plane at a current-clamped drain (u − u₀ = −M·(h − 1) to 7 %, item 8); the passive drain velocity is an O(Δx) residue (halving 240 → 480). A junction that mixes planes (density ↔ velocity) would sit between the density row and its −M-scaled copy, 18.4 dB apart at this bias — not 44 dB. Open.
3. **Bias fork.** Every row is at the ratio bias; the analytic-bias leg (07-31's) is not re-run. G4 is a ratio of two gains at one bias; 08-01 ratio-bias measured +0.4 to +1.2 dB on the single-cell gain between conventions, so a G4 transfer to the analytic leg is in-model and open.
4. **Grid, scheme and cfl.** G4 falls 0.05–0.08 per doubling and the passive transfer rises 0.4 dB; first-order convergence is assumed, not established (08-02 §4 measured sub-first-order floors, fitted p = 0.55–0.89; 08-06's fitted order straddles 1), and the N = 720 rung was run only at −6 / −3 dB on m = 30. 08-13's ghost-centre clamp-placement term — the released scheme's first-order boundary error, measured on the phase leg — acts on **both** coupled clamps here (cell 1's drain and cell 2's source), and its size on a two-cell chain is not separated from the grid ladder; the cfl law 08-13 reproduced is visible in Table 7: at fixed N = 240, cfl 0.2 / 0.4 / 0.8 moves G4_A(J = −1) to 1.774 / 1.581 / 1.485, the stage-2 peak floor to -8.35 / -4.70 / -3.37 dB, the slot-mode floor to -11.65 / -6.26 / -3.49 dB, cell 1's own floor to -16.69 / -11.83 / -8.89 dB and the residue to -8.11 / -5.70 / -4.21 dB (bias held at the cfl-0.4 value). **Every key and verdict is a released-cfl-path (cfl = 0.4) statement**, as 08-12's are.
